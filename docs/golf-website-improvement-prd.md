Summary
The working build at localhost:3000 is already structured around golf, so this PRD is about
accuracy and experience, not restructuring. The build has Golf, Course, Membership,
Clubhouse and Hotel pages, but it was written before the 15 September masterplan and
says the routing is still underway.
It asks the web team for five changes to that build.
1. Bring the copy into line with the masterplan. The contradictions are listed under
Current site.
2. Add a Masterplan page: an interactive plan built from the drawing, with the survey
contours as a layer.
3. Place the new renders on the Course, Clubhouse, Hotel and Villas pages and in the
Gallery.
4. Make the scroll experience immersive without slowing the site.
5. Replace the exterior renders with terrain-checked versions once survey heights are
available.
The main risk is terrain. The renders show nearly flat ground, while the survey shows
dunes of 50 to 60 m with a valley more than 40 m below them. The fix is set out under The
terrain problem.
Current site
The working build has the right pages; its problems are statements the masterplan now
contradicts or supersedes, and the absence of any site plan. Twelve pages were read on
10 November 2026: Home, Golf, The Course, Membership, Clubhouse, Hotel, The Villas,
Investment, Gallery, Location, FAQ and Enquire. Lifestyle and Insights were not re-read.
The menu sits behind a Menu button and lists Golf (The Cour
se, Membership, Clubhouse), Investment, The Villas, Hotel, The Estate, Location, Lifestyle,
Gallery, Insights, FAQ and Enquire.

The terrain problem
The renders cannot be made accurate from the contour PDF, because the PDF does not
say how high anything is. Its lines have no height labels, so neither an image generator nor
a person can tell a dune crest from a hollow, or a 5 m rise from a 50 m one.
Image generators also do not read vector contour drawings as terrain. They need a picture
of the ground shape from the same camera position as the render.

Survey heights received, 10 November. The DXF of the 14 September drawing holds the
contours with height labels, so the terrain is now known. It is published as the Island Rock
Terrain Model, with a height map for guiding renders. An earlier estimate made from the
unlabelled PDF was wrong about the valley and has been replaced.
The contours are 1 m apart and labelled 13 to 91. The lowest line, 13, runs along the
beach, so heights below are given above the beach.
The frontal dunes reach about 50 to 60 m. The valley floor behind them is only 3 to 8 m
above the beach.
Hole 1 runs from about 37 m to 41 m and hole 2 climbs to about 53 m. Hole 3 then falls
to a green at about 5 m.
Holes 4 to 15 lie on the valley floor. Holes 16 to 18 and the driving range climb back to
between 13 and 27 m.
The clubhouse sits at about 35 m and the hotel at about 20 m. The highest ground,
about 77 m, is in the north-west corner.
Steps 2 to 5 above can now start. The survey is measured from a local reference point on
the interior dune ridge, given about 42 m, not from sea level. Height differences are exact;
the height of the 13 m line above the sea is still unknown.
The fix, in order
1. Get the survey with heights from the surveyor or from MWG. Any one of these works:
the LiDAR point cloud (LAS or LAZ), a DEM (GeoTIFF), or the DWG or DXF with
elevation on each contour. The minimum is the contour interval plus a few spot
heights.
2. Build one 3D terrain model of the site from it, with the 15 September routing, roads,
stands, clubhouse and hotel placed on it.
3. For each exterior render, set a camera in the model at the real position and export a
depth map and a plain shaded view.
4. Regenerate the render using that depth map as the guide, or hand the model to a
visualiser. The dunes then sit where the survey puts them.
5. Check each new render against the model view from the same camera before it goes
on the site.
Which renders are affected
Highest risk: the aerials (IMG_2198, 2208, 2194, 2197, 2199). They show wide areas of
ground and all of it is flat.
Medium risk: any view that shows the sea from ground level, including interiors with
ocean views. Whether the sea is visible from a building depends on its height and the
dunes in front of it.

Until the heights arrive
The contour lines can still be shown on the masterplan as a layer. They are the surveyor's

lines crowd together the ground is steepest. No 3D view should be published before the

Goals and non-goals
Goals
A first-time visitor understands within the first screen that this is a golf estate with a
hotel on the Mozambican coast.
Every fact on the site matches the 15 September masterplan, the survey, or a signed-
off data sheet.
The terrain is shown truthfully: correct plan, correct scale, correct dunes.
The site feels immersive on desktop and phone without becoming slow.
Enquiries arrive tagged by interest: residence, hotel, golf or investment.
Non-goals
Rebuilding the site on a new platform. The existing code and routes are kept.
A hotel booking engine or tee-time booking.
A new logo or brand identity. The Island Rock Golf Estate logo on the masterplan is
used as is.
Hole-by-hole content before the course designer supplies par and lengths.
Site structure
